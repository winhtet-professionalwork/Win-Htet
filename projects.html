Smart Solar Drip Irrigation System

Project Type: Senior Engineering Capstone

Period: Still working

Research Areas: Smart Agriculture, Sensors, Automation, and Renewable Energy

Project Overview

This project focuses on developing a low-cost, solar-powered smart drip irrigation system for sustainable agriculture. The proposed system uses soil moisture sensors to monitor soil conditions at multiple locations within a garden and supports data-based irrigation decisions to improve water management.

An Arduino collects soil moisture measurements and communicates with a Raspberry Pi. The Raspberry Pi supports data processing and a web-based monitoring platform, allowing users to remotely monitor soil conditions and system information. The overall design integrates sensor technology, embedded systems, communication, and solar energy.

System Components

  • Solar power system
  • Capacitive soil moisture sensors
  • Arduino microcontroller
  • Raspberry Pi 3 Model B
  • Web-based monitoring dashboard
  • Drip irrigation and water pump control system

My Responsibilities

My primary responsibilities include wireless communication, sensor data acquisition, technical control system development, and integration of hardware with the web-based monitoring platform. I also contribute to data visualization and the development of data-based irrigation decision support.

Project Goals

The goal is to develop an affordable and practical irrigation system that uses real-time soil moisture information to support efficient water management and sustainable agricultural practices, particularly for small-scale agricultural applications.

Undergraduate Mathematics Research

Applying PageRank Algorithms to Analyze Diplomatic Connectivity Among G0 countries

Period: Still working

Research Area: Applied Mathematics and Computational Analysis

Research Overview

This undergraduate research project focused on applying the PageRank algorithm to analyze diplomatic connectivity among G20 nations. I developed and implemented the PageRank algorithm using MATLAB and constructed directed graph models to represent diplomatic relationships among nations. Through this research, I investigated the convergence properties of iterative numerical algorithms and applied concepts from linear algebra, probability, and graph theory to study network connectivity. I also presented my research findings at an undergraduate research seminar.
